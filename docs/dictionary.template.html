Every symbol the library draws, what it means, and when to reach for it.

A thermal network says three kinds of thing. Nodes are places that have a temperature. Branches are paths heat takes between two places. Sources are heat crossing into or out of one place from outside the network. Everything below is one of those three.

The drawings are in heat-transfer notation rather than circuit notation: a path is a hatched box, not a zigzag. The reason is that a box has interior room for a mechanism and a zigzag does not, and in this vocabulary the interior of a box states what the heat is crossing. Section hatching is solid material, streamlines are a moving fluid, wave arrows cross an empty box because radiation needs no medium, and opposed hatching is two solids meeting. One rule generates all four, so it is learned once.

  • Boxes resist, arrows carryA box is a resistance: heat crosses it and drops in temperature. An arrow is a rate: heat being delivered or carried, with no resistance stated.
  • The labelOne text block per symbol, on one side of the path. Your words are the top line at every orientation; under them the symbol and its value, joined by an equals sign.
  • Two kinds of subscriptOn a resistance it is structural and the library sets it — it names the physics. On T, C, P and q it is identity, and it is yours to set.
  • No colourThe whole set is monochrome, which leaves colour free for temperature maps and path highlighting.
